Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky

Fyodor Dostoevsky was a famous Russian novelist and philosopher. He is considered one of the greatest writers in Russian and world literature.

About Fyodor Dostoevsky

Fyodor Dostoevsky was born in 1821 in Moscow and passed away in 1881 in St. Petersburg. A turning point in his life was his arrest for participating in a political circle, followed by a mock execution and four years of hard labor in exile. His main contribution to world culture was the creation of profound psychological novels, such as "Crime and Punishment" and "The Brothers Karamazov,"
which revolutionized literature by deeply exploring the human soul, questions of faith, and morality. Severe life hardships and his struggle with illness formed the foundation of his unique literary style and philosophical views.

Important Events

  1. November 11, 1821 — Birth in Moscow
  2. April 23, 1849 — Arrest for political activity
  3. December 22, 1849 — Mock execution and reprieve
  4. 1850-1854 — Hard labor in Siberia
  5. February 9, 1881 — Death in St. Petersburg

Key Contributions

  • Fyodor Dostoevsky wrote the great "Pentateuch" of novels and became a globally recognized classic author translated into hundreds of languages.
  • He became the ultimate explorer of the human soul by masterfully revealing hidden motives, dark sides, and internal conflicts.
  • His ideas shaped existentialism and psychoanalysis while directly influencing major thinkers like Nietzsche, Sartre, and Freud.
  • Dostoevsky developed the innovative polyphonic novel genre where each character possesses an independent voice and a fully realized philosophy.
